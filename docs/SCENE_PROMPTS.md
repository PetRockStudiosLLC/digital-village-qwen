# Scene Prompt Guide

A scene prompt sets the context for your multi-character conversation. Good prompts lead to engaging, natural dialogues.

---

## What is a Scene Prompt?

The scene prompt is a description of:
- **Where** the characters are
- **What** they're doing
- **Why** they're there
- **Mood**/atmosphere

It tells the AI characters the situation they're in, so they can react naturally.

---

## Basic Structure

A good scene prompt includes:

1. **Setting** - Where are they?
2. **Characters present** - Who's involved?
3. **Activity** - What are they doing?
4. **Goal/Tension** - What's happening or being discussed?

---

## Examples

### Example 1: Casual Conversation
```
Finn, Jake, and Shrek are sitting around a campfire in the Ooo Swamp. Finn is excited about a new adventure, Jake is being his chill self, and Shrek is grumpy but tolerating them. They are discussing what to do tomorrow.
```

### Example 2: Argument
```
Shrek and Donkey are stuck in traffic on the way to Far Far Away. Shrek is getting increasingly frustrated with Donkey's constant talking. Donkey keeps trying to make small talk to pass the time.
```

### Example 3: Emotional Scene
```
Finn finds Jake lying on the ground, injured after a battle. Finn is worried and scared, trying to help his best friend. Jake is trying to stay positive but clearly in pain.
```

### Example 4: Comedy
```
Shrek is at a job interview at a corporate office. He's completely out of his element. The interviewer is a very serious, professional type who doesn't know what to make of Shrek.
```

---

## Tips for Great Prompts

### Do:
- ✅ Name the characters explicitly
- ✅ Describe each character's mood/state
- ✅ Give them a reason to interact
- ✅ Include conflict or tension (even small)
- ✅ Set a clear location

### Don't:
- ❌ Be too vague ("They are talking")
- ❌ Leave out character moods
- ❌ Make it too long (keep it 1-3 sentences)
- ❌ Be too complex (one scene, not a whole movie)

---

## Status Effect in Prompts

You can hint at status effects in the scene:

**Drunk prompt:**
```
Shrek and Donkey are at a party. Shrek has had too much ogre punch and is feeling tipsy. Donkey is trying to keep him upright while dancing.
```

**Caffeinated prompt:**
```
Finn just drank three cans of Super Adventure Energy Drink. He's bouncing off the walls, full of manic energy, while Jake watches nervously.
```

## Prompt Templates

### Template 1: Gathering
```
[CHAR1], [CHAR2], and [CHAR3] are at [LOCATION]. [CHAR1] is [MOOD1], [CHAR2] is [MOOD2], [CHAR3] is [MOOD3]. They are [ACTIVITY].
```

### Template 2: Conflict
```
[CHAR1] and [CHAR2] are [SITUATION]. [CHAR1] is [FEELING] about it while [CHAR2] feels [FEELING]. They need to [GOAL].
```

### Template 3: Task
```
[CHARACTERS] need to [TASK]. There's a problem: [PROBLEM]. [CHAR1] wants to [APPROACH1] while [CHAR2] wants [APPROACH2].
```

## Generating Ideas

 Stuck? Try these formulas:

**"Two characters, one problem"**
```
[CHAR1] and [CHAR2] need to [TASK] but [PROBLEM]. They argue about the best way to do it.
```

**"Unexpected meeting"**
```
[CHAR1] runs into [CHAR2] at [LOCATION]. [CHAR1] is surprised because [REASON].
```

**"Wrong place, wrong time"**
```
[CHAR1] accidentally [ACTION] and [CHAR2] is not happy about it. [CHAR2] demands an explanation.
```

**"Betting on it"**
```
[CHAR1] and [CHAR2] make a bet about [TOPIC]. [CHAR1] is confident they'll win. [CHAR2] thinks otherwise.
```

---

## Debugging Bad Prompts

**Problem: Characters don't interact**
→ Add a reason for them to talk to each other

**Problem: Responses are too generic**
→ Add more specific character moods and traits

**Problem: Conversation ends too quickly**
→ Add tension or a goal they need to achieve together

**Problem: Characters act out of character**
→ Remind of character traits in the prompt
